— Overview

A clinic worth visiting, on a site that gave no reason to.

Luminous Laser Dentistry is a laser dentistry practice in Roatán, serving a mix of local and expat patients drawn to the island. The clinic's old site had no real landing page and no call to action — a visitor could land on it and leave with no clear next step, no sense of what made the clinic worth booking.

I redesigned and rebuilt the site solo in Wix Studio, the platform the clinic was already on. The new site opens with a clear value proposition aimed directly at its actual audience — pain-free, laser-based treatment, and a bilingual English-speaking team for Roatán's expat community — backed by a full sitemap covering treatments, the clinic's Smile For Life program, FAQs, and a location/contact page built for patients who don't already know the island.

At the center of the rebuild is a booking flow that routes appointment requests straight to the clinic's email — tested and working across both desktop and mobile.

Tools: Wix Studio · Figma

— Chapter 01

Speak directly to the patient who's actually looking.

Rather than generic dental-practice copy, the new homepage leads with who the clinic actually serves: "Pain-free laser dentistry, built for life on the island," backed by a subhead calling out 30 years of clinical experience and a bilingual, English-speaking team for Roatán's expat community.

From that first screen, a visitor has exactly two clear paths forward — Book a Consultation or Explore Services — instead of scrolling with no direction, which is exactly what the old site left people to do.

Homepage hero — direct value proposition, audience, and two clear CTAs
— Chapter 02

Answer the questions before they're asked.

A dental clinic serving visiting and expat patients gets the same handful of questions on repeat: do you treat visiting patients, is laser dentistry actually more comfortable, do you take walk-ins, where's the clinic. I built a dedicated FAQ page to answer these upfront, reducing the friction between "I have a question" and "I've booked."

The Contact page pairs a booking form with an embedded map pin — a small detail that matters more than it looks, since patients unfamiliar with the island need to actually find the clinic, not just know it exists.

FAQ — answering visiting-patient, comfort, and walk-in questions upfront
Contact — booking form paired with an embedded map for out-of-town patients
— Chapter 03

A booking flow that actually goes somewhere.

The old site gave visitors nowhere to go. I configured the site's booking flow so consultation requests route directly to the clinic's email — a working intake process instead of a dead end — and tested it end-to-end on both desktop and mobile before launch.

A CTA is only as good as what happens after someone clicks it.

Built responsive — tested on both

Patients are booking from their phones as often as their laptops, so both had to work cleanly.

A real clinic, a working booking flow, and a site that finally explains what they do.
